Sharing and Feedback

Below are my personal reflections and feedback following my participation in the First Cloud AI Journey (FCAJ) program:

I. Overall Evaluation

1. Overall Experience

My internship at First Cloud AI Journey (FCAJ) was a highly practical learning experience. Throughout the program, I not only gained knowledge about AWS and Cloud Computing but also developed skills in self-learning, technical research, problem solving, and communicating what I had learned.

What I appreciated most was that the program did not focus only on memorizing knowledge. Instead, interns were encouraged to explore topics independently and apply what they learned through exercises, workshops, and projects. This helped me understand the difference between knowing a technology and actually being able to use it to solve a problem.

2. Learning Environment

The FCAJ learning environment is open and encourages self-learning. During the internship, I was able to choose my own approach to problems, research documentation, and experiment with different solutions.

Being exposed to real cloud technologies also made the learning process more practical. Instead of studying theory only, I could deploy, test, observe errors, investigate their causes, and improve the implementation.

3. Support from Mentors and Team Admin

I highly appreciate the support from the Mentors and Team Admin throughout the internship. Their guidance helped me understand that when facing a technical problem, finding a quick fix is not always enough. It is also important to understand the root cause, evaluate different approaches, and choose an appropriate solution.

Being encouraged to research independently before asking for help also helped me become more proactive. I believe this is an important skill for working in a real engineering environment.

4. Knowledge and Skills Gained

During the internship, I had opportunities to strengthen and expand my knowledge in several areas:

  • AWS and Cloud Computing.
  • Serverless Architecture.
  • Storage and Databases.
  • Networking and APIs.
  • IAM and Cloud Security.
  • Docker and Containers.
  • Infrastructure as Code with Terraform.
  • Monitoring and Logging.
  • CI/CD and DevOps concepts.
  • Backend development.
  • Git and source-code management.

Besides technical knowledge, I also improved soft skills such as planning, documentation, problem presentation, information searching, and time management.

5. Self-Learning and Research

One of the most important skills I gained during the internship was self-learning.

Cloud technologies include many services and a large amount of documentation, so it is not possible to rely only on previously taught knowledge. In many situations, I had to read official documentation, study examples, experiment in a real environment, and analyze errors.

Through this process, I gradually developed a clearer approach to learning and problem solving:

Identify the problem → investigate the cause → read documentation → experiment → evaluate the result → document the knowledge.

6. Workshops and Learning Activities

The workshops helped me approach different topics step by step while also having opportunities for practical exercises rather than learning theory alone.

Completing exercises and worklogs throughout the program also helped me maintain a consistent learning process. Having to summarize what I had learned after each stage encouraged me to review my knowledge and identify areas that I did not fully understand.

7. Worklogs, Blogs and Share Back

I found the worklog and knowledge-sharing activities particularly useful.

At first, documenting what I had done could be time-consuming, but the process helped me organize my knowledge and remember it better. Explaining a topic to others also helped me discover areas that I had not fully understood myself.

This is a habit that I would like to maintain after completing the internship.

8. Teamwork and Communication

During the internship, I had opportunities to discuss solutions and share experiences with other members.

These activities helped me realize that software development is not only about coding ability. A good solution also depends on communication, reaching agreement, and presenting technical problems clearly.

9. Practical Project Experience

The exercises and projects throughout the internship helped me connect different areas of knowledge into more complete systems.

During the final stage, I had the opportunity to apply knowledge of AWS, backend development, databases, security, and DevOps to the CodExecute project. However, what mattered most to me was not simply completing the project, but learning how to choose technologies, design an architecture, deploy it, test it, and handle problems that occurred during implementation.

This helped me better understand how knowledge from different courses and technical areas can be combined to solve a practical engineering problem.

II. Additional Questions

1. What did I enjoy most during the internship?

What I enjoyed most was the opportunity to learn independently.

I was not simply given knowledge to memorize. I was also placed in situations where I had to research and solve problems myself. This made me more confident when approaching technologies or problems that I had never worked with before.

Being exposed to AWS and cloud technologies over an extended period also helped me better understand my potential career direction.

2. What was the most difficult part of the internship?

The biggest challenge for me was the breadth of knowledge and technologies involved. A single problem could involve application code, databases, networking, IAM, cloud infrastructure, or deployment at the same time.

At the beginning, it was sometimes difficult to determine where to start. However, throughout the internship, I gradually improved my ability to break a large problem into smaller parts and investigate them individually.

3. How did the internship change my learning approach?

Previously, I often focused on learning how to use a particular technology. After the internship, I became more interested in understanding why a technology should be used, what problem it solves, what its limitations are, and how it can interact with other components.

I also became more likely to read official documentation and use hands-on experiments to validate my understanding instead of relying only on tutorials.

4. Would I recommend FCAJ to my friends?

Yes. I would recommend FCAJ to students who want to learn about AWS, Cloud, Backend, or DevOps and are willing to invest time in self-learning.

In my opinion, one of the strengths of the program is that interns are not only expected to receive knowledge but also to research, practice, and share what they have learned.

III. Suggestions and Future Expectations

1. Suggestions for Improving the Program

Some areas that I think could be expanded include:

  • More regular weekly Live Q&A sessions.
  • Architecture or technical review sessions for projects.
  • More practical troubleshooting exercises.
  • Additional security and cost-optimization challenges.
  • More teamwork or mini-challenge activities.
  • More real-world AWS architecture case studies.

These activities could help interns better understand technical decision-making rather than focusing only on implementation.

2. Topics I Would Like to Explore Further

After the internship, I would like to continue learning more deeply about:

  • AWS and Cloud Architecture.
  • DevOps and CI/CD.
  • Kubernetes and Container Orchestration.
  • Cloud Security and DevSecOps.
  • Observability and Distributed Systems.
  • Infrastructure as Code.
  • Cost Optimization.
  • System Design.

These are also areas that I would like to develop further as part of my career direction.

3. Staying Connected with FCAJ

After completing the internship, I would like to stay connected with the FCAJ community and continue following its activities.

I would also be interested in participating in future workshops, technical sharing sessions, or community activities so that I can continue learning and sharing the knowledge I have gained.

IV. Acknowledgement

I would like to sincerely thank the Mentors, Team Admin, and the First Cloud AI Journey community for creating a positive learning environment throughout the internship.

I appreciate the time spent providing guidance, answering questions, and giving feedback that helped me improve both my learning approach and problem-solving skills.

The internship not only helped me strengthen my knowledge of AWS and Cloud Computing but also helped me develop a stronger self-learning mindset, research skills, and a more systematic approach to technical problems.

These experiences will be valuable foundations for my continued academic and professional development.
